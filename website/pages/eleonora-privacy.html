Eleonora privacy policy

Eleonora (formerly called Eudora) is a free desktop and phone email program made by Planet Networks, Inc. This policy covers Eleonora on Windows, Mac, Linux, iPhone, and Android, and the earlier Eudora versions.

Effective November 18, 2026. Developer: Planet Networks, Inc., Newton, NJ. Questions: software@planet.net.

In short

Eleonora is a program that runs on your own computer or phone. It reads your mail, contacts, and calendars from your own accounts so that you can use them, and it keeps them on your device. Planet Networks runs no server that receives, stores, or sees your data. Nothing about you or your mail is sold, shared, or shown to Planet Networks or anyone else. There are no ads, no analytics, and no telemetry. Eleonora's source code is open (github.com/robertlboyle/eleonora), so anyone can check that it does only what this page says.

Google user data

When you add a Gmail or Google Workspace account, you sign in on Google's own page, and Google asks you to let Eleonora use:

  • Gmail (https://mail.google.com/): your messages and their labels, to show, search, file, label, sort, mark, delete, and send your mail, and to bring changes you make in Eleonora back to Gmail (and changes made elsewhere into Eleonora).
  • Google Contacts (…/auth/contacts): your contacts, to show them in Eleonora's Address Book, complete addresses as you type, and save your edits, new contacts, and deletions back to Google.
  • Google Calendar (…/auth/calendar): your calendars and events, to show them in Eleonora's calendar, and to make, change, answer, and delete events you choose (with a Google Meet when you ask for one).
  • Your Google account's email address, to know which account you signed in to.

Eleonora uses this data only to give you the email program's own features, on your device, at your request. It is not used for advertising, not sold, not shared with or shown to Planet Networks or anyone else, and not used to develop, improve, or train artificial-intelligence or machine-learning models, Eleonora's or anyone else's. No person at Planet Networks reads it, because none of it ever reaches Planet Networks.

Limited Use: Eleonora's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Microsoft 365, Outlook.com, IMAP, and POP3

The same commitments apply to every other kind of account. For Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com, Eleonora uses Microsoft Graph, with your permission given on Microsoft's own sign-in page, to read and change your mail, mailbox folders, shared mailboxes you can open, Online Archive, contacts, calendars, and Microsoft To Do tasks, to send mail you write, and to suggest names from your organization's directory as you type an address. For IMAP, POP3, SMTP, CardDAV, and CalDAV servers, it signs in with the user name and password you type. In every case the data goes only between your device and your own provider.

Where your data is kept

  • On your own device only, in your Eleonora library (a folder in your user account): a copy of your mail, folders and labels, contacts, calendars, tasks, rules, and settings.
  • Sign-ins and passwords are kept in your operating system's own protected store: Windows Credential Manager, the macOS or iOS Keychain, Linux's own secret store, or, on Android, files sealed with a key in the Android Keystore. Eleonora never sees your Google or Microsoft password: you type it on their own pages.
  • Your mail stays on your provider's servers too. Eleonora downloads a copy; your provider remains the source of truth.
  • Backups are made only when you ask, to a file you choose, encrypted with a password if you set one. Google and Microsoft sign-ins are never put in a backup.

What else Eleonora connects to

Besides your own mail providers and their sign-in pages, Eleonora goes online only for what you use or switch on, and sends only what each needs:

  • Spamhaus blocklists, only if you enter your own Spamhaus key: the sending server's IP address and the sender's domain of arriving mail, to check for spam.
  • Domain registration dates (RDAP, from the domain registries) for the domains of senders who write to you, so the spam filter can notice domains registered days ago.
  • Pictures in a message, fetched from their senders' servers only when you ask, or for senders you chose to always show pictures from.
  • Place suggestions for the text you type into an appointment's location: Google Maps, with your own Google Maps key, or else OpenStreetMap's Photon service.
  • Online meetings you add to an appointment: Zoom, only after you sign in to your own Zoom account; Teams and Google Meet through your Microsoft or Google account.
  • Unsubscribe, when you choose to unsubscribe: the sender's own unsubscribe address.
  • Twos, only if you set it up and send a task there: that task's text.
  • The update check, on software.planet.net, which sends only Eleonora's name and version. You can switch it off.
  • Downloads you need: Eleonora's natural voices from software.planet.net, when you choose to download them, and, on a computer without text recognition of its own, the reading models of the open-source ocrs project, the first time Eleonora reads text in a picture. Nothing about you is sent with them.
  • Settings sync, if you turn it on: your settings and filing, encrypted with a key only you hold (shown to you once as a recovery phrase), stored in your own mailbox (a hidden Gmail label, an IMAP folder, or a Microsoft 365 folder), where your other devices read them. Without your key nobody, Planet Networks included, can read them.

None of these services receives your mail, your contacts, or your calendar.

Security

Every connection to your providers and to the services above is encrypted (TLS), and certificates are always checked. Data on your device is protected by your device's own drive encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on a Mac, or your phone's built-in encryption) and by your user account. Programs and plugins that use Eleonora's local API get only the permissions you grant, and everything they do is logged for you to see.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Your data stays on your device until you delete it. You can choose how much mail is kept on each computer.
  • Forget Sign-ins removes every saved sign-in and password from your device.
  • Removing a shared mailbox, or a contacts and calendar (CardDAV / CalDAV) account, removes its data from Eleonora on your device and leaves it on the server.
  • Uninstalling Eleonora on Windows offers to delete your library, Eleonora's folders, and its saved sign-ins and passwords (after you type DELETE). On other systems, delete Eleonora's folder in your user account. On a phone, removing the app removes its data.
  • Revoking access: you can remove Eleonora's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, and to your Microsoft account at myaccount.microsoft.com (Apps and services, or for a personal account, account.live.com/consent/Manage). Eleonora then can no longer reach that account.

Children

Eleonora is not directed at children and collects nothing about anyone.

Changes to this policy
