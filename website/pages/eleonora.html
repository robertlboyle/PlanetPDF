Eleonora

A fast desktop email program for very large mailboxes: every account in one place, search that keeps up with decades of mail, and sorting that keeps your inbox for people. Eleonora is the new name of Eudora, after Eleonora's falcon, one of the fastest birds there is.

Eleonora {{eleonora_version}} Eudora is now Eleonora. Eleonora downloads a copy of your mail and leaves the mail on your provider's servers (except POP3), and it keeps itself up to date. Its source code is open, so anyone can check that it does only what it says: github.com/robertlboyle/eleonora. Please send what you find to software@planet.net.

What it's for

Most email programs slow down or give up when a mailbox holds hundreds of thousands of messages, or thirty years of mail. Eleonora handles tens of millions of messages and stays fast: lists open at once, search answers in about a second, and scrolling never waits. It looks modern, while its Classic Eudora look keeps the menus, commands, and shortcuts familiar to anyone who loved the classic Eudora.

New in Eleonora

A new name and a falcon

Eudora becomes Eleonora, with a new falcon icon. Your mail, settings, and saved passwords move over by themselves the first time it starts, and backups, plugins, and settings sync from Eudora still work.

A graceful new look

The Eleonora look: the list and the message on rounded cards, a soft sidebar, quiet icon buttons, roomier rows, and a soft blue tint for what's chosen, in light and dark. Classic Eudora's look is still there, its icons included.

One Inbox at the top

Every one of your own accounts' Inboxes together, at the top of the mailboxes, with what was sorted out of them lately right below.

Contacts from a message

Beside a message's sender: copy the address, write to them, or add or edit their contact. In a contact, write, call, or copy with one click.

Export messages as files

Choose messages anywhere, or a whole search, and export them as .msg or .eml files in one go, in the background, named by their date and time.

Accounts your way (2.0.1)

Remove an account from Settings, the Mailbox menu, or its right-click menu (nothing changes on the server), switch an account between IMAP and POP3, and rename or delete a mailbox from the Mailbox menu, no right-click needed on a Mac.

Drafts on the server (2.0.2)

Save Draft also puts the draft in the account's Drafts folder on Microsoft 365, Outlook.com, Gmail, or an IMAP server, so it's on your phone and other computers too; a draft there has Continue Writing.

Copy to a Phone (2.0.4)

File ▸ Copy to a Phone shows a QR code; scan it with Eleonora on your phone and your whole Eleonora comes over the Wi-Fi, sealed with a one-time code, with no backup file on either side. A copy that's cut off goes on from where it stopped.

Over a USB cable, and calendar files (2.0.5)

Copy to a Phone lists a phone's USB tethering first, so the copy goes over the cable instead of the Wi-Fi. Calendar files (.ics, .cal) ask which of your calendars the event goes on, and File ▸ Open File opens them.

Import from Classic Eudora, your way (2.0.8)

Point it at your old Eudora folder, where it is, and tick exactly what comes in: any of your mailboxes (with each message's status, priority, and label), your personalities as working accounts, filters, nicknames, signatures, and labels. It can make Eleonora look and work like classic Eudora, with its menus and keys and ">" quoting, and you can run it again any time to add more.

Typing made easier (2.0.8)

Address completion finds any part of a name or address and shows full names, an × takes a wrong address out for good, Undo brings back a paragraph deleted by mistake, and an up arrow pins any folder to the top.

Nothing fails quietly (2.0.12)

An account that can't be checked, or a message that can't be sent, tells you why every time, with a way to fix it. Troubleshoot Sending and Troubleshoot Receiving try the usual ports, encryption, user names, and sign-ins for an IMAP or POP3 account and save the settings that work.

One set of mailboxes, and Views your way (2.0.12)

One computer that keeps everything can show one set of mailboxes for every account, as classic Eudora did, with In, Out, Junk, and Trash. Views come from a gallery and can be renamed or made from any search, and Settings can be searched by the words you'd use. Draft with AI and the connector for AI agents are there only if you set them up.

A sturdier move from classic Eudora (2.0.11)

Importing a classic Eudora folder now reads every message's status, priority, and label from its .toc file, keeps messages apart that classic had run together, and never stops on one unreadable file. The reading pane can be turned off, and a double-click opens a message in a window of its own.

Join Zoom meetings in one click (2.0.10)

Zoom links open straight in the Zoom app, appointments with a Zoom or Webex link get Join the meeting, Attach lists the files you used most recently, an invitation can be ignored without answering, and pictures shown in messages can be kept in an Embedded folder of their own, as classic Eudora did.

Tidy attachments, as classic Eudora did (2.0.9)

With attachments kept as files, Empty Trash can send each file to the Recycle Bin once no message uses it any more (off at first, as in classic Eudora). And the text cursor is now easy to see on light pages.

Search that does what you mean (2.0.7)

Group conditions with parentheses or Advanced Search's groups, leave groups out with NOT, search any header (x-my-header="Some Value"), and find attachments by type: filetype:pdf, filetype:spreadsheet. Search results show which mailbox each message is in.

Attachments as files, if you like them that way (2.0.7)

An option keeps every attachment as an ordinary file in folders you design (by year, account, sender, or type), as classic Eudora's Attach folder did, while every message still reads exactly as it came. Also: edit a message's subject, Send Again on the toolbar, and spelling in the language you choose.

Mailing lists accept your mail

Text goes in the simplest character set it fits, so list servers that refuse some encodings take your messages.

Every account, one program

Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com

Your mailbox, its Online Archive, and the shared mailboxes you can open, all in one place. Sign in through Microsoft's own page; Eleonora never sees your password.

Gmail and Google Workspace

Mail, contacts, and calendars through Google's own sign-in. Gmail's labels show as folders, and changes go both ways.

IMAP and POP3

Any other mail provider, or your company's own server, with SMTP for sending. POP3 is there for older mailboxes. Contacts and calendars from CardDAV and CalDAV servers (iCloud, Fastmail, Nextcloud, cPanel) too, both ways.

Your old mail comes along

Import classic Eudora mailboxes, mbox files, and saved messages. A message stored in several places, such as old Eudora mail you once moved to Microsoft 365, is kept once. And you can always go back: export everything, rules included, as classic Eudora's own files, or keep a classic copy up to date.

Your copy, your choice

For each account, choose whether your changes to mail, calendar, and contacts go back to the server or stay only on your computer, for a local copy that's independent of the server's.

One inbox, or each account

Look at every account's mail together, or open one account's own folders. Rarely used accounts and folders can be hidden.

Send as any of your addresses

Pick the From address when you write, as in the classic Eudora: any of your accounts, or a shared mailbox you may send for.

Fast with tens of millions of messages

Instant search

Search every message you've ever kept, on your own computer. Outlook-style words work too, such as from:, subject:, hasattachment:yes, and received:last week. A mistyped search gets a suggestion.

Lists that open at once

Folders open in a blink, even with hundreds of thousands of messages, and stay ready in the background. New mail slides in without moving what you're reading.

Works offline

Read, file, flag, and write mail without a connection. Changes go to the server when you're back online, and downloads pick up again by themselves after a dropped connection.

Sort by any column

Click a column heading to sort by it, and add any header you like as a column. Check boxes choose messages, or every message a search found.

Advanced Search

Classic Eudora's Find Messages window is back: build a search from rows of what to look for, all or any of them, without learning any search words.

Search where you choose

Leave any mailbox out of search with one click on its magnifying glass: all shared mailboxes, a whole account, or one folder, then put back just the one you want. One more click searches everything.

An inbox for people

Marketing, notifications, and lists sorted out

Newsletters and sales go to Bulk ▸ Marketing, receipts and alerts from services to Notifications, mailing lists to Lists, each big sender in a folder of its own. Mail from people stays in the inbox.

A spam filter that learns

It learns from what you mark as spam or not spam, checks who really sent a message, and can use Spamhaus blocklists. A Maybe Spam view shows what it wasn't sure about.

Fraud caught and made harmless

Fake DocuSign, Microsoft, bank, and shipping notices are flagged in red. Their links stop working and their pictures never load, so one click can't do harm.

Red Alerts

Mail you must never miss stands out: shut-off and past-due notices, government and tax notices, security advisories, and urgent mail from the people you write to.

Your own rules

The classic Eudora filters, complete: move, copy, label, set priority, forward, or reply, on arriving mail or on any folder. Try a rule on a folder before it moves anything.

Other Recent Mail

Everything that was sorted out of your inbox lately, spam left out, in one view: the last 48 hours, or 72 on Mondays to take in the weekend. You choose the hours.

See why

Every sorting decision can be explained: which rules a message hit and why. Switch any rule off, or give it exceptions.

Views that find things for you

Receipts, invoices, and payments

Receipts, Invoices, Renewals, Payments Received, Payments Sent, and Bank Transactions gather themselves, grouped by who sent them. A payment and its receipt are linked when their amounts match.

Travel, Legal, and more

Travel bookings, legal mail, government mail, new accounts, and sign-in codes each have a view. Text inside PDF files and pictures is read, so a receipt's total is found even in an attachment.

Subscriptions in one place

Every newsletter and mailing list you get, how often each writes, and an Unsubscribe button that follows the sender's own unsubscribe link.

Statistics

How much mail you get and send, your busiest days, and how much of it is personal, notifications, marketing, or spam.

Calendar, contacts, and To Do

Calendar

Microsoft 365, Google, and CalDAV calendars side by side: Day, Week, Month, and Agenda. Make appointments with a Teams, Google Meet, or Zoom meeting and place suggestions; answer invitations, or propose another time. Delete any event, one meeting of a series, or the whole series, and search your events from the same search box as your mail.

Trips and events, added for you

Flights (both ways of a round trip), trains, and tickets to shows and conferences go on your calendar from their confirmation emails, with the boarding pass or ticket attached. Hotel stays and car rentals are offered.

Invitations

Pending invitations in one place, with any conflicts shown. An invitation that's really spam is removed without a reply, so the sender never learns your address works.

Address Book

Your Microsoft 365, Google, and CardDAV contacts together. Edit, add, or delete, and the change goes back to the server. Find and merge duplicates field by field (undoable), move or copy contacts between accounts, and export to vCard or CSV.

To Do

Flag any message as a To Do and open it again from the task. Tasks sync with Microsoft To Do, so they're on your phone too, and the search box finds them.

Writing mail

Signatures for each address, spell check, pasted pictures, Send Later, drafts saved as you type, and voice dictation into any message. HTML or plain text with one switch, strikethrough and highlight, Undo and Redo, Paste as Plain Text, and classic Eudora's ">" quoting if you prefer it.

Made to be yours

Eleonora or Classic Eudora

The Classic Eudora look looks and works like the Eudora you remember, down to its menus and shortcuts. Or pick the Eleonora look, or one in the style of Outlook, Thunderbird, Gmail, or Yahoo Mail, each with that program's menus and shortcuts. Dark mode included.

For everyone, blind and low vision included

Works fully with screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, Narrator, VoiceOver) and by keyboard alone, with text sizes for every window, high-contrast colors, Read Aloud, and natural voices that run on your computer. Classic Eudora's new-mail sounds are there too.

Settings in one place, and a real manual

Every setting is in one Settings window, a page at a time. A complete user manual, with search and an index, is under Help (F1).

Private by design

Your mail stays on your computer and with your mail provider. Eleonora collects nothing about you, has no ads and no tracking, and pictures from the web load only for senders you choose.

Back up and move

Back up everything (mail, accounts, rules, and settings) to one file, encrypted with a password if you like, and restore it on a new computer, Windows, Mac, or Linux. A new computer's first screen offers the restore, and a restore can replace the library you have open.

Clear progress

Long jobs say exactly where they are, such as "Importing message 120,000 of 1,412,000", and every background job can be paused or stopped from one status bar.

Scripts and plugins

Every function works from a command line and a local API, and plugins can add commands. Each program gets only the permissions you grant it, and everything it does is logged.

Updates itself

Eleonora checks for new versions, downloads them, and checks each one's signature before it installs. You choose how often it looks, or turn it off.

Downloading and installing

Windows
(Intel / AMD)		Eleonora-Setup.exe, version {{eleonora_version}}: for Windows 10 or 11 on a PC with an Intel or AMD processor (most PCs; Settings ▸ System ▸ About ▸ System type says "x64-based processor"). It installs for you alone without an administrator, or for everyone on the computer if you choose. Installed over Eudora, it takes Eudora's place and keeps your mail and settings. Windows may warn that it doesn't know the program: choose More info ▸ Run anyway.
Windows on ARMEleonora-Setup-arm64.exe, version {{eleonora_version}}: for Windows 11 on a PC with an ARM processor (Snapdragon laptops, Surface Pro X and 11, other Copilot+ PCs; System type says "ARM-based processor"). It runs natively there: faster, and easier on the battery, than the Intel / AMD version under Windows' emulation. Installed over that version, it takes its place and keeps your mail and settings.
MacEleonora.dmg, version {{eleonora_mac_version}}: one app for Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, macOS 11 or newer, signed and notarized by Apple. Open it and drag Eleonora into Applications; it then updates itself.
LinuxVersion {{eleonora_linux_version}}, for Ubuntu 24.04, Debian 13, Fedora 40, or newer. For PCs (x86-64): AppImage, .deb, .rpm. For ARM64 computers (such as a Raspberry Pi 5): AppImage, .deb, .rpm. The AppImage runs without installing: allow it to run as a program (in its Properties, or chmod +x Eleonora-*.AppImage) and start it. Install the .deb with sudo apt install ./eleonora_*.deb, the .rpm with sudo dnf install ./eleonora*.rpm. Eleonora updates itself the way you installed it: the AppImage replaces itself, and the .deb and .rpm ask for an administrator's password. Installing the .deb or .rpm also adds Eleonora's package repository, so your system's updates offer new versions too.
Already on Eudora?Eudora updates itself to Eleonora. Your mail, accounts, rules, settings, and saved passwords come along.
Android (beta)Download Eleonora for Android (beta, version {{eleonora_android_version}}, 51 MB), for Android 7 or newer. It's an early test version: not everything works yet, and it doesn't update itself, so download it again for a newer one (it installs over the old one and keeps your mail). To install it, open the downloaded file on your phone and allow your browser to install apps when Android asks. It keeps its own copy of your mail, downloaded from your accounts; you choose how much (the last 30 days, a year, everything, or up to a size).
iPhoneThe iPhone version is being built.
Your mailEleonora keeps its own copy of your mail in your user folder. Uninstalling it never deletes that copy, or anything on your mail provider's servers.
