Accessibility: Screen Readers, Keyboard, and Contrast

PlanetPDF can be used with a screen reader, with the keyboard alone, with a Windows contrast theme, and with larger text. This chapter tells you how each part of the program works that way.

Using a screen reader

PlanetPDF works with Windows Narrator, NVDA, and other screen readers on Windows, and with VoiceOver on a Mac. Every button, box, list, and panel has a name the screen reader says, including the buttons that show only an icon.

  • Menus hold every command, so you can do everything from the menu bar (Alt, then the arrow keys). The shortcuts are listed in Keyboard Shortcuts.
  • Messages in the status bar at the bottom of the window ("Saved", "Added the bookmark") are read out as they appear.
  • Long jobs (exporting, recognizing text, printing) say what they are doing when they start, and then every few seconds: "Exporting to Word. Reading page 7 of 10".
  • Opening a file says its name, how many pages it has, and the page you are on. Turning pages says the new page number.

Reading a page's text

The page itself is a picture, so a screen reader can't read it directly. View ▸ Zoom ▸ Reflow (Ctrl+4) shows the page's text as ordinary text the screen reader can read:

  • The text is in reading order. Headings are marked as headings, so your screen reader's heading keys (H in NVDA's browse mode, for example) jump between them.
  • Each picture on the page is mentioned as "[Picture]".
  • A page with no text (a scan, for example) says so. Scan & OCR ▸ Recognize Text finds the words in a scan; after that, Reflow can read them. See Scans.
  • The arrow keys move a cursor through the text, so you can read by character, word, and line. Page Down at the end of the text, or Ctrl+Page Down anywhere, goes to the next page (Page Up / Ctrl+Page Up: the previous one).
  • Ctrl+4 again goes back to the page.

Tip: View ▸ Read Out Loud reads pages aloud with PlanetPDF's own voices, if you prefer that to your screen reader.

Filling in forms with the keyboard

The fields on the page can't be reached by a screen reader, so fill them in from the Fill Form panel instead:

  1. Open the document. When it has form fields, the Fill Form panel opens on its own (otherwise choose View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Fill Form Panel).
  2. Press F6 until you reach the Fill Form panel. Its list has every field in order, with its type and value; required fields end with an asterisk (*).
  3. Choose a field with the arrow keys and press Enter. The keyboard goes to the box below the list, named after the field: a text box, a check box, or a drop-down list of the choices.
  4. Type or choose the value. In a text box, Enter saves it and goes on to the next field. Check boxes and choices are saved as soon as you change them.

Each value you enter is one step of Edit ▸ Undo. Signature fields and read-only fields say why they can't be filled in there.

Comments with the keyboard

Comments are read and answered in the Comments panel (open the Comment tool, or View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Comments Panel). Press F6 until you reach it.

  • From the Search comments box, Down Arrow goes to the first comment. Each comment is read in full: its kind, its author, its page, its date, its words, and its replies. The page shows the comment as you reach it.
  • Up and Down Arrow go to the previous and next comment; Home and End go to the first and last.
  • Enter (or F2) edits the comment's words; Ctrl+Enter saves them and Esc cancels.
  • R goes to the reply box; type the reply and press Ctrl+Enter to post it.
  • Delete deletes the comment (Ctrl+Z brings it back), and the menu key or Shift+F10 opens its menu (Edit, Delete, Flatten).

Placing stamps, signatures, and text with the keyboard

Stamps, signatures, initials, Add Text, symbols, sticky notes, and text boxes can be placed without a mouse:

  1. Choose what to place from its menu or tool bar (for example Tools ▸ Stamp, then a stamp from the Stamp menu, or Sign Yourself on the Fill & Sign bar). For stamps and signatures, the keyboard goes to the page at once; for the other tools, press F6 until you reach the page.
  2. Press Enter. It is placed in the middle of the part of the page that shows, and a screen reader says so. Added text opens for typing straight away.
  3. Move it with the arrow keys (one point at a time), or Shift and an arrow key (ten points). A screen reader says where it is, in inches from the left and the top of the page. Delete removes it.

Tip: To place it on another page, go to that page first (Page Down, or Ctrl+Shift+N and its number), then press Enter.

Working with the keyboard

F6 moves the keyboard to the next part of the window, and Shift+F6 to the previous one (also Window ▸ Next Pane and Previous Pane), in this order: the main toolbar, the open tool's bar, the text format bar and the Find bar when they show, the page (or Reflow's text, or the Organize Pages grid), the Pages or Bookmarks panel, each panel that is open, and the tool strip on the right. A screen reader says which part you reached.

  • In a toolbar, the Left and Right Arrow keys go from button to button (Up and Down on the tool strip); Home and End go to the first and last. Enter or Space presses the button or opens its menu, and Down Arrow opens a button's ▾ menu. Esc goes back to the page.
  • Tab and Shift+Tab move between the boxes and buttons of a panel or dialog, in the order you see them: top to bottom, and left to right.
  • In the Pages panel, each thumbnail is read as "Page 3 of 10" (with its page label, when the document has them).
  • A ring in your highlight color shows which toolbar button the keyboard is on, and a ring around the page shows when the keyboard is on the page.
  • On the page, Page Down / Page Up turn pages, Home / End go to the first and last page, and Ctrl+Shift+N goes to a page by number.

Contrast themes

When Windows uses a contrast theme (Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Contrast themes), or a Mac has Increase contrast on, PlanetPDF uses its colors, whatever you chose in View ▸ Display Theme. Icons are drawn in the theme's text color, and the tool that is switched on is marked in the theme's highlight color with a border. PlanetPDF follows the setting as soon as you change it; icons that were already showing change color the next time PlanetPDF starts.

The pages of your documents always show as they will print. To see them larger, zoom in (Ctrl+=), or use Reflow (Ctrl+4), which shows the text larger as you zoom.

Larger text

Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ Appearance ▸ Text size (on a Mac, Preferences is in the PlanetPDF menu) makes the text of the menus, toolbars, panels, and dialogs larger: Normal, 125%, 150%, 175%, or 200%. It applies to every window as soon as you click Save. PlanetPDF also grows with the display scale you set in Windows (Settings ▸ System ▸ Display ▸ Scale).

PDF files for people who use screen readers

This chapter is about using PlanetPDF itself. PlanetPDF doesn't yet add the "tags" that let a screen reader read a PDF file well in other programs (Acrobat's Accessibility tool, PDF/UA). Text you export with File ▸ Export To ▸ Text (Accessible) or to Word keeps the words in reading order.
