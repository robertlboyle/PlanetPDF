Comments, Notes, and Stamps

This chapter covers marking up a document without changing its pages: notes, highlights, text boxes, shapes, drawings, attached files, and stamps. It also covers replies, the Comments panel, and Check Spelling. Comments are saved as standard PDF comments, so Adobe Acrobat and other PDF programs show them too.

The Comment tool

Everything you add with the Comment tool sits on top of the page. You can move it, change it, or delete it at any time, and the page underneath stays as it was.

  1. Choose Tools ▸ Comment, or click Comment on the tool strip down the right side of the window.
  2. The Comment bar opens under the main toolbar, and the Comments panel opens on the right.
  3. Click a tool on the bar, then use it on the page.
  4. When you are done, click the Select arrow on the main toolbar, pick another tool, or click Close at the right end of the Comment bar.

The pointer shows what a tool does. Tools that place text (notes, Insert Text at Cursor, text boxes) show an I-beam; tools that draw a box or a shape show a crosshair. Highlight, Underline, Strike-Out, Replace Text, and Text Callout show the I-beam over text and the crosshair elsewhere.

Marking a block of text with a box: with Highlight, Underline, Strike-Out, or Replace Text, start dragging off the text (where the pointer is a crosshair): a box follows the mouse, and every word it touches is marked when you let go. Starting the drag on a word selects from that word to where you let go, as usual.

The two quick buttons on the main toolbar, Add a sticky note and Highlight text, open the Comment tool with that tool already on.

Tip: A tool stays on until you choose another one, click the arrow, or close the Comment bar, so you can add several highlights or boxes in a row. The button of the tool that's on is highlighted. Esc only drops what you are drawing at that moment; the tool stays on.

Notes and sticky notes

The Sticky Note tool works like a highlighter. The pointer is a text cursor.

  • A note on words: drag across the words. They are highlighted, and a note box opens beside them. Type your note.
  • A sticky note anywhere: click a spot on the page (away from text, or a click that catches no words). A note icon goes there and its box opens beside it. Type your note.

Your note is saved when you click somewhere else. A note left empty isn't added. The note box is joined to its highlight or icon by a line, and it stays open until you collapse it. See Note boxes.

Tip: New note boxes go to the right edge of the page, level with what they belong to, so they cover as little text as possible. When the item is at the right edge, the box goes to the left edge, or under the line when neither side has room.

Highlight, underline, and strike through

  1. Click Highlight, Underline, or Strikethrough on the Comment bar.
  2. Drag across the words, as you would to select text. The marks follow the lines of text, in reading order.

Highlights are yellow, underlines blue, and strike-throughs red until you choose another color. A click that catches no words does nothing. These marks belong to the words under them, so they can't be dragged elsewhere; delete one and mark the words again instead.

To add a note to a highlight, select it (in the Comment tool) and type in its note box, or double-click it. See Changing a comment's words.

Note: Scanned pages are pictures; there are no words to mark until the text is recognized. See Scans, Text Recognition, and File Size.

Replace Text

Suggests new words in place of old ones, as a proofreader would. The page itself doesn't change.

  1. Click Replace text.
  2. Drag across the words to replace. They are crossed out in blue.
  3. A note pad opens beside them. Type the new words and click elsewhere to finish.

If you leave the note pad empty, nothing is added.

Insert Text at Cursor

  1. Click Insert text at cursor.
  2. Click between two letters or words. PlanetPDF puts a caret mark (^) at the nearest gap between characters.
  3. Type the words to insert in the note pad and click elsewhere.

Text boxes and text comments

Text Box puts text in a box with a border; Add text comment puts text on the page with no box around it. Both work the same way:

  1. Click the tool.
  2. Click on the page for a standard-width box that grows as you type, or drag a box to the width you want.
  3. Type. Press Enter for a new line. Click elsewhere to finish.

Text wraps inside the box, and the box grows taller to fit it. To change the font, size, bold, italic, underline, strike-out, or color, use the Aa ▾ menu on the Comment bar (see Text format). With some words selected while you type, B, I, U, and S change just those words.

To change a text box later, double-click it and type. With a text box selected, a small bar over it has A− and A+ (smaller and larger text, also Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+]), a color button, and a delete button. A text box you empty is deleted.

Text Callout

A text box with a line and arrow pointing at something on the page.

  1. Click Text Callout (one of the drawing tools).
  2. Press the mouse button on the spot you are pointing at, drag to where the text should go, and let go.
  3. Type the text in the box and click elsewhere.

The arrow points at where you started. Double-click the callout to change its words.

Drawing tools

The drawing tools draw shapes on top of the page. What you see while drawing is what you get.

ToolHow to draw
Line, ArrowDrag from one end to the other. The arrowhead is at the end where you let go.
Rectangle, OvalDrag from one corner to the opposite corner.
PolygonClick each corner. To finish, double-click, press Enter, or click the first corner again.
CloudLike Polygon, but with a cloudy border, the usual way to circle a change on a drawing.
Connected LinesClick each point. To finish, double-click or press Enter. The shape stays open.

While drawing a polygon, cloud, or connected lines, Esc drops the shape you were drawing. Rectangles, ovals, polygons, and clouds can be filled with a color: see Color, line thickness, and opacity.

Collapse Drawing Tools

To save room on the Comment bar, click Collapse Drawing Tools (the last drawing-tool button). The eight drawing tools fold into one button:

  • Click the button to use the drawing tool you used last. The button shows that tool's icon.
  • Click its ▾ to choose any drawing tool, or Expand Drawing Tools to put them all back on the bar.

PlanetPDF remembers which way you like it.

Pencil drawings and erasing them

Click Pencil and drag to draw freehand. Each stroke is a drawing of its own.

To erase part of a pencil drawing:

  1. Click Erase free-form drawing. The pointer becomes a circle the size of the eraser.
  2. Drag over the parts to erase.
  3. Press + for a bigger eraser or − for a smaller one. (It's the same eraser size as Edit PDF's Eraser.)

Each stroke of the eraser is one undo step. It only erases pencil drawings made in PlanetPDF; it never touches the page or other comments. To remove a whole drawing, select it and press Delete.

Color, line thickness, and opacity

The three buttons at the right of the Comment bar set how comments look.

  • Color ▾ (the paint bucket; the bar under it shows the current color):
    • Line Color… sets the color for the tool that's on (each tool remembers its own), and recolors the selected comment.
    • Fill Shapes turns filling on or off for rectangles, ovals, polygons, clouds, text boxes, and callouts; Fill Color… picks the fill.
    • Opacity: 100%, 75%, 50%, or 25%.
  • Line thickness ▾: 0.5 pt to 12 pt.
  • Aa ▾: the text format for text boxes, text comments, and callouts.

Line color, thickness, and opacity also change the selected comment, as one undo step. The fill color applies to what you draw next.

Text format

The Aa ▾ menu holds the text format bar: the font list (fonts used in this document first, then the standard PDF fonts, PlanetPDF's own, and the fonts on your computer), the size, B I U S, and the color. It changes:

  • the text you are typing (or just the selected words);
  • the selected text box or callout, when you aren't typing;
  • otherwise, the next text box or callout you add.

Fonts you use are saved inside the PDF, cut down to the letters used, so the comment looks the same on other computers.

Attach File

  1. Click Attach file.
  2. Click where the paperclip should go.
  3. Choose the file. It is stored inside the PDF.

The tool goes back to the arrow after each file. Double-click a paperclip to save a copy of its file. PlanetPDF never opens or runs an attached file.

Note boxes

Notes, highlights, and the other comments that carry words show them in a note box, drawn in a pale shade of the comment's color. The header shows who wrote it and when. A line joins the box to its comment.

ToDo this
Edit the wordsClick inside the text.
Move the boxDrag its header or its edge.
Resize itDrag any corner. The opposite corner stays put. The box never gets shorter than its text.
Collapse itClick – in its header.
Open it againClick the comment it belongs to (in the Comment tool, select it).
ReplyClick Reply in its header. See Replies.

The pointer changes over a box to show what a press will do: a text cursor, a four-way arrow for moving, or a diagonal arrow at a corner. Boxes grow as you type.

Moving or resizing a box is an undo step. Opening or collapsing one isn't, and doesn't count as a change to the file, but whether each box is open is saved with the file, as Acrobat does.

Tip: When you are typing in a note or text box, the first click elsewhere only finishes the typing. The next click moves a box or uses the tool.

Note boxes are hidden while Edit PDF or Prepare Form is open. To see comments in a list instead of boxes, see Comments in the panel or on the page.

Replies and threads

A reply answers a comment, as in Acrobat. Replies show under the comment, oldest first, in its note box and in the Comments panel.

  • In a note box: click Reply in the header, type in the note pad that opens under the box, and click elsewhere.
  • In the Comments panel: click the comment's card, type in the Reply… box, and click Post.

To change a reply, click its words in the note box (or double-click them in the panel). To delete it, empty its words, or use its card's menu. Replies are undoable like everything else. Other PDF programs that support threads (such as Acrobat) show them as replies.

Who wrote a comment

Every comment and reply carries its author's name and the date it was last changed. The name is Your name from Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ General; if that's blank, your Windows login name is used, as Acrobat does.

The author changes only when a comment's own words are edited: then it is signed by whoever edited them. Moving, resizing, recoloring, replying to, opening, or collapsing a comment keeps its author as it was, even a blank one. Undo puts the old author back.

The Comments panel

The Comments panel lists every comment in the document. It opens with the Comment tool; you can also show it with View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Comments Panel.

  • At the top: the number of comments, a Search comments box (it matches the words, authors, and kinds of comments and replies), and a ⋯ menu.
  • Comments are grouped by page (PAGE 3 (2)) and listed top to bottom.
  • Each card shows the kind of comment, the author and time, its words, and its replies.
  • Each card's ⋯ menu has Edit and Delete (stamps and other placed items have Flatten instead of Edit).
  • Double-click the words on a card to edit them right there, then click Save or Cancel.
  • With the keyboard: Down Arrow from the search box goes to the cards, Enter edits, R replies, and Delete deletes. See Comments with the keyboard.

Going from the list to the page and back

Click a card and the page scrolls to its comment, centers it, and marks it briefly; its note box opens. The selected card has a blue frame and a reply box.

Click a comment on the page and its card is selected in the panel.

Comments in the panel or on the page

In the panel's ⋯ menu choose:

  • Show Comments in Boxes on the Page (the usual way): note boxes on the page, as described above.
  • Show Comments in This Panel: no boxes on the page. Clicking a comment on the page opens the panel on its card instead.

Selecting, moving, and resizing comments

With the Comment tool open, click any comment to select it (with a tool on, you can still click the selected comment to move it).

  • Move: drag it. You can even drop it on another page.
  • Resize: drag a corner handle.
  • Nudge: the arrow keys move it 1 point; Shift+arrow, 10 points.
  • Delete: press Delete, choose Edit ▸ Delete, or right-click it and choose Delete.
  • Right-click for Edit Text…, Larger Text / Smaller Text (text boxes), Flatten (placed items), and Delete.

Highlights, underlines, strike-throughs, and replaced text stay with their words and can't be moved. Comments made by other programs can have their words edited and can be deleted, but not moved.

Every change is one undo step: Edit ▸ Undo (Ctrl+Z).

Changing a comment's words

Double-click a comment (or right-click it and choose Edit Text…). Text boxes and callouts are edited right on the page; notes, highlights, and shapes in their note box. Click elsewhere to finish. A shape or highlight that had no words gets a note box when you give it some.

Flattening

Flattening makes a stamp, added text, or a signature part of the page itself: it can't be moved or changed afterwards, and every program prints and shows it.

  • One item: right-click it and choose Flatten (can't be moved afterwards), or use its card's ⋯ menu in the Comments panel.
  • A whole page or document: open Tools ▸ Fill & Sign and choose More ▸ Flatten Page or Flatten All Pages.

Notes, highlights, shapes, and text boxes stay comments; they aren't flattened. Flattening is undoable until you save.

Stamps

A stamp sits on top of everything on the page, like a rubber stamp. (A watermark goes underneath: see Editing Text and Pictures.)

  1. Choose Tools ▸ Stamp, or click Stamp on the tool strip. (The Comment bar has the same Stamp ▾ menu.)
  2. Click Stamp ▾ and pick a stamp. It follows the pointer.
  3. Click on the page to place it at its usual size, or drag a box to size it. Esc cancels.

Placing a stamp is one and done: the arrow comes back, and the new stamp is selected so you can adjust it.

The Stamp menu

  • At the top, the stamp you used last: click it to place it again.
  • Your favorites.
  • Dynamic, Sign Here, Standard Business, and Custom, each showing pictures of its stamps.
  • Add Current Stamp to Favorites / Remove Current Stamp from Favorites (the current stamp is the one used last).
  • Show Stamp Names: names under the pictures.
  • Paste Clipboard Image as Stamp Tool, Custom Stamps ▸, and Stamps Palette.

Company Confidential and your company name

Company Confidential prints your company's name over the word CONFIDENTIAL. Type the name in Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ General ▸ Company name. While it's blank, the stamp says just CONFIDENTIAL. PlanetPDF never puts a company name into a document unless you type one.

Custom stamps

The Custom Stamps ▾ button on the Stamp bar (also in the Stamp menu) has:

  • Create Text Stamp…: type a Name, the Main text and an optional Second line. The buttons under them insert {date}, {time}, {user} (your name), and {company} (your company name), which are filled in each time you place it. Choose the Color, the Style (Acrobat style shaded tile, or outlined text with a border, which also offers Font, Bold, and Border), Rotation, and Opacity. A preview shows the result. Click Save.
  • Create from Image…: choose a PNG or JPEG picture (a seal, a logo, a scanned stamp) and name it.
  • Manage…: the Stamps and Seals window lists every stamp, with New Text Stamp…, Import Seal Image…, Edit…, Duplicate, Delete, and Place on Page. Built-in stamps can't be edited or deleted, but Duplicate makes an editable copy.

Your stamps are kept in your PlanetPDF profile, so they follow you on a terminal server. They are listed under Custom.

Stamps Palette

Click Stamps Palette for a small window with every stamp. Choose a category at the top (Favorites, Dynamic, Sign Here, Standard Business, or Custom), click a stamp, then click on the page. The palette stays open for the next one.

Pasting a picture as a stamp

Copy a picture in any program, then choose Stamp ▾ ▸ Paste Clipboard Image as Stamp Tool. The picture follows the pointer; click or drag to place it. It is placed once and isn't added to your stamps (use Create from Image… for that).

Moving and stretching stamps

While the Stamp tool (or the Comment tool) is open, click a stamp to select it.

  • Drag it to move it, even to another page.
  • Drag a corner to make it bigger or smaller; it keeps its proportions.
  • Hold Shift while dragging a corner to change its proportions (wider, narrower, taller). The stamp stretches to fill the box in every PDF program and when flattened.
  • Press Delete to remove it.

When Fill & Sign is open, stamps can't be selected, so they don't get in the way of filling in.

Check Spelling

Check Spelling finds misspelled words in English in:

  • comments and their notes,
  • the values typed into form text fields,
  • the page's own text (on unless you untick Also check the page text), including the hidden recognized text over scanned pages.

It skips words in ALL CAPITALS (usually acronyms), words with digits in them, web and e-mail addresses, and words in your dictionary and word lists.

  1. Choose Edit ▸ Check Spelling ▸ In Comments, Fields & Editable Text….
  2. Click a word in the Misspelled list. The line it's in shows below, with suggestions.
  3. Pick a suggestion (or type the right word in the box) and click one of:
    • Change: fix this word (double-clicking a suggestion does the same).
    • Ignore: skip it here.
    • Ignore All: skip this word everywhere until you close PlanetPDF.
    • Add to Dictionary: never mark this word again.
    • Go To: show the word on the page, outlined in red.
  4. Click Close when done.

Every change is an undo step.

Changing words in the page's own text

Changing a word in the page's text rewrites that line of the PDF, the way Edit PDF does. PlanetPDF first tells you what will happen and asks:

  • Usually the line keeps its font, size, and color; the words after the change may shift a little.
  • PDFs often hold only the letters they use. If the line's font lacks a letter of your correction, the whole line is drawn in the closest font PlanetPDF has, at the same size and color. You can restyle it in Edit PDF afterwards.

Words in recognized (OCR) text over a scan: Change corrects the hidden text PlanetPDF recognized, so search and copy find the right word; the picture stays as it is. Text that another program recognized can't be changed (the button is grayed).

Dictionaries and word lists

Choose Edit ▸ Check Spelling ▸ Edit Dictionary… (or Dictionaries… in the Check Spelling window).

  • Word lists: PlanetPDF comes with lists of Academic & Science, Business & Finance, Companies & Brands, Computing & Programming, Legal, Medical, Networking & Telecom, People's Names, and Places. Words in a ticked list are never marked. Untick a list to switch it off.
  • Import Word List… adds your own list: a text file with one word per line, or a Hunspell .dic file. Remove List takes an imported list away.
  • Your own words: the words you added with Add to Dictionary. Type a word and click Add, or select one and click Remove.

Names keep their capitals: a list word written with capitals inside, such as "JavaScript" or "iPhone", is marked when written another way ("Javascript"), with the right spelling suggested. A word in a list in lowercase is accepted however it's capitalized. Plurals and possessives of list words are accepted too. Your words and lists are kept in your PlanetPDF profile.
