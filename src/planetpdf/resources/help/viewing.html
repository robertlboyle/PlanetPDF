Reading and Finding

This chapter is about reading documents: moving between pages, zooming, laying pages out on the screen, finding words, copying text and pictures, having a document read aloud, and opening the files attached to a PDF. Nothing in this chapter changes the file.

Moving between pages

Scroll with the mouse wheel or the scroll bar, or use any of these:

  • The page number box on the main toolbar: type a page number and press Enter. Beside it, "/ 10" shows how many pages there are. Ctrl+Shift+N (View ▸ Page Navigation ▸ Go To Page…) puts the cursor straight in the box.
  • Previous Page and Next Page, the up and down arrow buttons on the main toolbar.
  • The keyboard, while the page area has the focus (click a page first):
KeyWhat it does
Right Arrow / Left ArrowNext page / previous page
Down Arrow / Up ArrowScroll down / up a little
Page Down / Page UpScroll a screenful (in Single Page View and Two Page View, turn the page)
Home / EndFirst page / last page
Alt+Left / Alt+RightPrevious view / next view

When an item on the page is selected (a comment, a signature, placed text), the arrow keys nudge it instead (1 point, or 10 points with Shift). Click an empty part of the page to deselect it first. The same keys also work while the Pages panel has the focus.

View ▸ Page Navigation

  • First Page (Home), Previous Page (Left Arrow), Next Page (Right Arrow), Last Page (End).
  • Go To Page… (Ctrl+Shift+N): type the page number in the box.
  • Previous View (Alt+Left) and Next View (Alt+Right) go back to where you were before a jump (after following a link or a bookmark, for example) and forward again, like a web browser's Back and Forward.

Zooming

The quickest ways to zoom:

  • Zoom In (Ctrl+= or Ctrl++) and Zoom Out (Ctrl+-), or the + and − buttons on the main toolbar. They step through the same zoom levels each time, as in Acrobat: 12.5, 25, 33, 50, 67, 75, 100, 125, 150, 200, 300, 400, 600, and 800%.
  • Hold Ctrl and turn the mouse wheel: the page zooms around the mouse pointer.
  • The zoom box on the main toolbar: choose Fit Width, Fit Page, or a percentage, or type a percentage and press Enter. View ▸ Zoom ▸ Zoom To… (Ctrl+Y) puts the cursor in this box.

Marquee Zoom

  1. Choose View ▸ Zoom ▸ Marquee Zoom.
  2. Drag a box around the part of the page you want to see. It zooms so that area fills the window, and the tool turns off.

Press Esc before dragging to cancel.

Dynamic Zoom

Choose View ▸ Zoom ▸ Dynamic Zoom, then press on the page and drag up to zoom in or down to zoom out, around the place you pressed. It stays on until you press Esc or choose Select or Hand.

Loupe Tool

The Loupe shows a close-up of one spot in a small window, while the page stays at its zoom.

  1. Choose View ▸ Zoom ▸ Loupe Tool. The Loupe window opens, showing the middle of the page.
  2. Click (or drag) on the page to choose what it shows. A red box on the page marks that spot.
  3. Drag the Magnification slider to change the enlargement (100 % to 6400 %; it starts at 400 %).

Press Esc or close the Loupe window to stop.

Pan & Zoom

View ▸ Zoom ▸ Pan & Zoom opens a small window with the current page and a red box showing the part that's in the main window. Drag the box (or click elsewhere on the small page) to move around. Its buttons turn pages and zoom in and out, and it shows the zoom percentage. This is handy at high zoom, where it's easy to lose your place.

Page Display: how pages are laid out

View ▸ Page Display (also the Page Display and Zoom button on the main toolbar) chooses how pages are arranged:

LayoutWhat you see
Single Page ViewOne page at a time. Page Down, Next Page, or scrolling past the bottom turns to the next page.
Enable ScrollingOne page wide, all pages in one long column you scroll through (the usual layout).
Two Page ViewTwo pages side by side, a pair at a time; Page Down turns to the next pair.
Two Page ScrollingTwo pages side by side, scrolling continuously.
Four Page Scrolling (2×2)Two pages side by side, and Zoom to Page Level fits two rows: four pages on the screen at once.

In the page-at-a-time views, the mouse wheel scrolls within the page and, at the top or bottom edge, turns the page (one page per flick).

Show Gaps Between Pages

View ▸ Page Display ▸ Show Gaps Between Pages puts a gray space between pages. Turn it off to show the pages touching.

Show Cover Page in Two Page View

In the two-page layouts, View ▸ Page Display ▸ Show Cover Page in Two Page View shows the first page on its own, like a book's cover, so that even pages are on the left and odd pages on the right.

Automatically Scroll

View ▸ Page Display ▸ Automatically Scroll (Shift+Ctrl+H) scrolls the document by itself, so you can read without touching the mouse. It scrolls forward (backward if you start it on the last page).

  • + scrolls faster, − slower (the status bar shows the speed).
  • Down Arrow and Up Arrow change the direction.
  • Esc, a click, or Shift+Ctrl+H again stops it.

In the scrolling layouts it stops at the end of the document. In Single Page View and Two Page View it stays on each page as long as scrolling past it would take, then turns the page.

Rotate View

To read a page that's sideways, turn the view: View ▸ Rotate View ▸ Clockwise (Ctrl+Shift++, which on most keyboards is Ctrl+Shift+=) or Counterclockwise (Ctrl+Shift+-), or the two rotate buttons on the main toolbar. With Shift held these keys always turn the view; without it they zoom. Every page turns by 90°. This only changes how PlanetPDF shows the pages: the file isn't changed, nothing is saved, and it isn't an undo step. The view is upright again when you next open the file.

To turn pages in the file itself (so they stay turned for everyone), use Edit ▸ Rotate Clockwise or the Organize Pages tool; see Organizing Pages.

Read Mode and Full Screen Mode

  • View ▸ Read Mode (Ctrl+H) hides the toolbars and panels, leaving the menu bar and the pages. Choose it again (or press Ctrl+H) to bring everything back as it was.
  • View ▸ Full Screen Mode (Ctrl+L) fills the whole screen with the pages: no menus, bars, panels, or window frame. Press Esc (or Ctrl+L) to leave it. The page-turning keys work as usual.

Reflow

View ▸ Zoom ▸ Reflow (Ctrl+4) shows the current page's text alone, rewrapped to the width of the window, in reading order. It's useful for reading small print on a narrow window. Pictures and layout aren't shown; words broken by a hyphen at the end of a line are joined again.

Headings are shown as headings, and each picture as "[Picture]". A screen reader reads Reflow's text (see Reading a page's text).

  • Left Arrow / Right Arrow show the previous / next page (while a screen reader runs they move the cursor through the text instead).
  • Page Down at the bottom (or Page Up at the top) also turns the page, and so do Ctrl+Page Down / Ctrl+Page Up anywhere.
  • Ctrl+4 again, or opening another tool, goes back to the pages.

A scanned page without recognized text shows "(no text on this page)"; see Scans, Text Recognition, and File Size.

Rulers, grid, guides, and cursor coordinates

These help you place and line things up. They're on View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Rulers & Grids, and none of them print or go into the file.

  • Rulers (Ctrl+R): inch rulers along the top and left of the pages, measured from the top-left corner of the page under the mouse.
  • Grid (Ctrl+U): lines every inch, with lighter lines every quarter inch.
  • Snap to Grid (Shift+Ctrl+U): things you draw or move, and guides, land on the nearest quarter inch. (Freehand drawing, typed text, and stamps you're placing don't snap.)
  • Guides (Ctrl+;): shows or hides the guides.
  • Clear Guides: removes them all.

Adding a guide

  1. Turn on Rulers (Ctrl+R).
  2. Press on the top ruler (for a horizontal guide) or the left ruler (for a vertical one) and drag onto the page.
  3. Let go where you want the line.

Guides last until you close the document or choose Clear Guides.

Cursor Coordinates

View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Cursor Coordinates shows, at the right of the status bar, the page the mouse is over and where, in inches from the page's top-left corner ("Page 2 X: 1.25 in Y: 3.50 in").

Page Controls

View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Page Controls ▸ Show Page Controls adds a small dark bar floating at the bottom of the pages, with previous / next page, the page number ("3 / 10"), and zoom out / in. It's handy when the toolbars are hidden.

The Pages panel (thumbnails)

The Pages panel shows a small picture of every page, with its number (or its page label) underneath. Open it with the Pages button at the edge of the window, View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Page Thumbnails, or F4 (see The Pages and Bookmarks panel).

  • Click a thumbnail to go to that page. The page you're on is selected as you scroll.
  • The arrow keys, Home, and End move through the pages while the panel has the focus.
  • Drag the panel's edge to make the thumbnails bigger or smaller.
  • Right-click a thumbnail to rotate, insert, extract, or delete pages. Dragging thumbnails reorders the pages. These change the document; see Organizing Pages.

The Bookmarks panel

Bookmarks are a document's table of contents: named places you can jump to. Open the panel with the Bookmarks button at the edge of the window or View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Bookmarks. A file that has bookmarks opens with the panel showing.

  • Click a bookmark to jump to its place. In Single Page View and Two Page View the page turns to it.
  • Click the arrow beside a bookmark to show or hide the bookmarks under it.
  • Alt+Left (Previous View) takes you back to where you were.

Adding, renaming, moving, and deleting bookmarks, and making them automatically from the document's headings, are in Organizing Pages.

Links in documents

When the mouse is over a link, the pointer becomes a pointing hand and a tip says where it goes. Click it to follow it:

  • A link to another place in the document jumps there (Alt+Left comes back).
  • A link to a web page asks Open this page in your web browser? first, showing the site and the whole address. Click Yes only if you trust where the document came from. An email link asks before starting an email to the address shown.
  • For your safety, PlanetPDF opens only web (http, https) and email links. Links to other files, to shared folders on other computers (file:, \\server), or that start programs aren't followed; PlanetPDF says so instead. A form button with a web address follows the same rules.

To add or change links, see Editing Text and Pictures.

Finding words

To find a word or phrase in the open document:

  1. Choose Edit ▸ Find… (Ctrl+F), or click the magnifying glass on the main toolbar. The Find bar opens with the cursor in the Find in document box. If you had selected a few words on the page, they're already filled in.
  2. Type what you're looking for and press Enter.

Every match is highlighted, the current one more strongly, and the bar shows how many there are ("2 of 15"); "No matches" when there are none. The search starts from the page you're on.

In a long document the bar counts as it goes ("Searching page 37 of 120… 12 matches"). The first match is shown as soon as it's found, you can keep working meanwhile, and Stop (or Esc) ends the search and keeps the matches found so far.

  • Enter, F3, or the down-arrow button: next match.
  • Shift+Enter, Shift+F3, or the up-arrow button: previous match.
  • Match case: only matches with the same capital and small letters.
  • Whole words: "art" doesn't match "start".
  • Esc or the ✕ button closes the bar and clears the highlights (while a long search is running, Esc stops it first).

A phrase is found even when it breaks onto the next line. Edit ▸ Advanced Search (Ctrl+Shift+F) opens the same Find bar.

Note: Find searches the document's text. It can't find words in a scanned page until the page's text is recognized (see Scans, Text Recognition, and File Size), text drawn as shapes, or text in hidden layers. To find and black out sensitive information such as Social Security numbers, use the Redact tool's Find Text & Redact (see Protecting and Redacting).

Search More Tools

Can't remember which menu a command is on? Choose Edit ▸ Search More Tools…, type part of its name (for example "watermark", "rotate", or "OCR"), choose it in the list, and press Enter to run it.

Selecting and copying text

With the Select arrow on (the main toolbar), drag across words on the page to select them. The selection follows the reading order across lines. Then:

  • Press Ctrl+C (or Edit ▸ Copy) to copy the text; the status bar says how many characters were copied. Paste it into any program.
  • Or right-click for Copy, Highlight Selected Text (adds a highlight comment), Add Bookmark (a bookmark named with the text, at its place), and Find….

Right-click ▸ Select Text Tool turns on a tool that only selects text (an I-beam pointer), so you don't pick up comments or signatures by accident; it stays on until you choose another tool. Scanned pages have text to select only after their text is recognized.

Take a Snapshot

To copy part of a page as a picture (a chart, a logo, a signature block):

  1. Choose Edit ▸ Take a Snapshot.
  2. Drag a box around the area. A picture of it, as the page shows it (comments included), goes onto the clipboard, and the status bar gives its size. The tool then turns off.
  3. Paste the picture into an email, a document, or an image program.

Press Esc before dragging to cancel. Edit ▸ Copy File to Clipboard copies the whole PDF file instead, to paste into a folder or an email.

Read Out Loud

PlanetPDF can read a document aloud, page by page, in reading order.

  1. Choose View ▸ Read Out Loud ▸ Activate Read Out Loud (Shift+Ctrl+Y). The reading buttons appear on the main toolbar, and the voice starts loading (the status bar says "Please wait…" while it gets ready; the first time can take a few seconds).
  2. Go to the page where you want to start.
  3. Choose Read This Page Only (Shift+Ctrl+V) or Read to End of Document (Shift+Ctrl+B). When reading to the end, the pages follow along as they're read.
CommandShortcutToolbar button

Pause / ResumeShift+Ctrl+CPause; it shows Play while paused or stopped (Play when stopped reads to the end from the current page)

Choose Activate Read Out Loud again to turn it off and hide the buttons.

Scanned pages are read only after their text is recognized.

Voices, speed, and volume

While Read Out Loud is on, the main toolbar has a Voice menu (choose a male or female voice; the change starts with the next sentence) and a volume slider (it changes at once). For more, choose More Read Out Loud Settings… from the Voice menu, or Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ Read Out Loud:

  • Voice engine:
    • Automatic (the usual choice): Kokoro when it's installed and fast enough on this computer, otherwise Piper (or the system's voices).
    • Kokoro: the most natural-sounding voices; needs a reasonably fast computer.
    • Piper: very good voices that are fast on any computer.
    • Windows voices (on Windows) or macOS voices (on a Mac). On a Mac, the Premium voices you can download in System Settings ▸ Accessibility sound much better than the basic ones.
  • A voice for each engine.
  • Speed: from -10 (slowest) through 0 (normal) to 10 (fastest). Try Voice plays a sample.

All the voices work offline: nothing you read is sent anywhere.

Downloading the natural voices

The natural (Kokoro) voices are included in the full Windows installation. If they're missing (a smaller Windows installation, or the Mac), PlanetPDF reads with the system's voices and offers the natural ones:

  • The first time you activate Read Out Loud in a session, it asks whether to download them (about 354 MB, once). Click Download, or Not Now to keep the system's voices.
  • Or click Download Natural Voices (354 MB)… in Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ Read Out Loud.

The download shows how many MB have arrived and can be canceled. When it's done, Read Out Loud uses the new voices straight away. They're kept on this computer and survive updates.

Attachments and PDF Portfolios

A PDF can carry other files inside it: a spreadsheet with an invoice, the originals of a combined report. View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Attachments opens the Attachments panel, which lists them. It opens by itself when you open a file that has attachments.

  • Save As… saves a copy of the selected file to a folder you choose. PlanetPDF never opens or runs an attached file itself; open the saved copy only if you trust it.
  • Add… attaches files from your computer to the PDF.
  • Remove takes the selected file out of the PDF.

Adding and removing are changes to the document: they can be undone, and you save them like any other change.

A PDF Portfolio is a PDF made mainly to carry a set of files, with a cover page. When you open one, the panel's heading says "PDF Portfolio" and lists its files. To make a portfolio, see Creating, Converting, and Comparing.
