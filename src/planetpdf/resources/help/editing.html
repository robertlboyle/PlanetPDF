Editing Text and Pictures

This chapter covers the Edit PDF tool, which changes the page itself: retyping and moving text, changing its font, adding text and pictures, working with lines and boxes, links, and the Eraser. Every change is one step you can undo with Edit ▸ Undo (Ctrl+Z).

The Edit PDF tool

To start editing, do one of these:

  • Choose Tools ▸ Edit PDF or Edit ▸ Edit PDF.
  • Click Edit PDF on the tool strip.

The Edit PDF bar appears under the main toolbar and the Edit PDF panel opens at the side. To stop editing, click Close at the right end of the bar.

The Edit PDF panel

The panel at the side has three parts:

  • FORMAT: the text format controls for the selected text (see Changing the font, size, and color).
  • OBJECTS: Flip Vertical, Flip Horizontal, Rotate Left, Rotate Right, Replace Image…, Delete, and Shape Color… for the selected pictures and shapes.
  • SCANNED DOCUMENTS: Recognize Text (OCR)… for pages that are scans.

At the bottom, Show bounding boxes outlines every line of text and every picture you can edit, so you can see how the page is divided up.

Selecting what to edit

In Edit PDF, each line of text, each picture, and each line or box drawn on the page is an object you can select.

  • Click an object to select it. A frame appears around it.
  • Click a selected line of text again (or double-click it) to edit its words.

Text you added with Add Text, signatures, and initials can also be selected here and moved like any object.

Selecting several objects

  • Drag a box on an empty part of the page: everything the box touches is selected. Hold Shift while dragging to add to the selection.
  • Shift+click an object to add it.
  • Ctrl+Shift+click an object to take it out of the selection.

Moving, deleting, formatting, rotating, and flipping then apply to all of them at once, as one undo step.

Editing text in place

  1. Click the line to select it, then click it again (or double-click it, or right-click it and choose Edit Text).
  2. A box opens on the line with its text selected, in a matching font. Type the new text.
  3. Press Enter to finish, or click somewhere else. Esc cancels.

The new text goes on the same line, at the same size, color, and direction. PlanetPDF uses the line's own font when it has all the letters you typed; otherwise it uses the closest standard font (Helvetica, Times Roman, or Courier, in the same weight and style), or Noto Sans for letters those fonts don't have. Neighboring lines stay where they were.

Lines in several fonts. When a line's words use different fonts or styles ("Please read the terms carefully."), clicking any part of it opens the whole line, each word shown in its own font. Each word keeps its font when the line is written back, and words you type take on the font of the word they're typed next to (typed right after "terms", they're bold too). The line is laid out again from its start, so the words after a change move along.

Emptying the line and pressing Enter deletes it. You can also right-click a line and choose Delete Text.

Note: Edit PDF works one line at a time. A paragraph is several lines: retyping one line doesn't rewrap the rest.

Moving text, pictures, and shapes

  • Drag a selected object to move it. If several are selected, dragging any one of them moves them all.
  • The arrow keys move the selection 1 point at a time; with Shift, 10 points.

Moved text keeps its exact letters, font, size, and color, and the rest of the page is left as it was. Underlines and boxes drawn tightly around a line of text, and link areas over it, move with it.

Moving to another page

You can move one or several objects to a different page:

  • Drag them onto the other page in the page view and let go.
  • Or drag them over that page's thumbnail in the Pages panel: the view scrolls to that page, and you can drop them there. If you let go on the thumbnail itself, they land in the same place on that page.

Dropping them in the gap between pages leaves them on their own page. The move is one undo step.

Deleting

Select one or more objects and press Delete, or click Delete in the panel's OBJECTS section. You can also right-click a line (Delete Text) or a picture (Delete Image). A line's underline, tight box, and links go with it.

Changing the font, size, and color

When text is selected, or while you are typing added text, the text format bar appears on its own row under the tool's bar. The same controls are in the Edit PDF panel under FORMAT.

ControlWhat it does
Font listChoose a font. You can type part of a name to find it.
SizeChoose or type a size in points (4 to 144), then press Enter.
B I U SBold, Italic, Underline, and Strike-out.
ColorPick the text color.

The changes apply to every selected line, as one undo step.

  • Lines of the page's own text: you can change the font, size, color, bold, italic, and underline. Underline draws a thin line under the text in its own color; click it again to take the underline away. Strike-out isn't available for page text.
  • Text you added (Add Text): every control works, and you can select single words while typing to make just them bold, italic, underlined, or struck out.

The font list

The list shows, in this order:

  1. Fonts that come with the open document but aren't on your computer, marked "(in this document)".
  2. The standard PDF fonts, Helvetica, Times Roman, and Courier, which every PDF reader has.
  3. PlanetPDF's own fonts (Noto Sans, Noto Serif, Caveat, Dancing Script, Great Vibes).
  4. The fonts installed on your computer.

Each name is shown in its own typeface. If the list is slow to open, turn that off in Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ Performance (Show fonts in their own typeface in font lists); see Preferences.

Some fonts' licenses don't allow them to be put into a PDF. If you choose one, PlanetPDF says so and leaves the text as it was.

"This font doesn't have" some letters

A font that comes with a document usually holds only the letters the document already uses. If you choose such a font for text that needs other letters, PlanetPDF lists the missing letters and asks:

  • For text you added: Keep the font (the missing letters are drawn in the closest standard font), Use the closest font for all the text, or Cancel.
  • For the page's own text: Use the closest font, or Cancel.

Likewise, if such a font has no bold or italic version for those letters, PlanetPDF tells you so; choose another font to make the text bold or italic.

Adding text

  1. Click Add Text on the Edit PDF bar, or choose Edit ▸ Add Text. The pointer becomes an I-beam.
  2. Click where the text should go and type. Set the font, size, and color on the text format bar, before or while you type.
  3. Press Enter or click somewhere else to finish (Shift+Enter starts a new line). The text isn't left selected, so you see exactly where it sits. The tool stays on, so you can click again to add more text.

Added text stays easy to adjust (click it to select it first):

  • Drag it to move it (to another page too), or use the arrow keys.
  • Drag a corner handle to make it larger or smaller (the type size changes with it).
  • Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[ make the selected text larger and smaller.
  • Double-click it to retype it.

Added text is the same as Fill & Sign's Add Text. To make it a fixed part of the page, use More ▾ ▸ Flatten Page or Flatten All Pages on the Fill & Sign bar (see Forms: Filling In and Making).

Pictures

Adding a picture

  1. Click Add Image… on the Edit PDF bar, or choose Edit ▸ Add Image….
  2. Choose a picture file.
  3. The picture follows the mouse. Click to place it, or drag a box to give it a size. Esc cancels.

The new picture is selected, so you can move or resize it right away. The tool turns off after one picture.

Moving and resizing a picture

Select the picture, then drag it to move it, or drag a corner handle to resize it. Resizing keeps the picture's proportions.

Replacing a picture

Right-click the picture and choose Replace Image… (or select it and click Replace Image… in the panel), then choose the new file. The new picture is fitted inside the old one's box.

Rotating and flipping

Select one or more pictures or shapes and click Rotate Left, Rotate Right, Flip Horizontal, or Flip Vertical in the panel. Each one turns or flips about its own center.

Lines and boxes

Lines, boxes, and other shapes drawn on the page can be selected like text and pictures. You can:

  • move them (by dragging or with the arrow keys) and delete them;
  • rotate and flip them with the panel's OBJECTS buttons;
  • change their color: select them and click Shape Color… in the panel.

A page's background (a color or picture filling the whole page) isn't offered, so you don't select it by accident. Where a line of text and a shape overlap, a click selects the text.

Links

A link is an area on the page that opens a web page or jumps to another page when clicked.

Adding or changing a link

  1. On the Edit PDF bar, choose Link ▾ ▸ Add/Edit Web or Document Link.
  2. Drag a box where the link should be (usually over its words). To change an existing link, click it instead.
  3. In the dialog, choose:
    • Open a web page: and type the Address. www.example.com becomes https://www.example.com, and an e-mail address becomes a link that starts a new message.
    • Go to a page in this document: and choose the Page.
  4. Click OK. When changing a link, Delete Link removes it.

The Link tool stays on for the next link; click Select (the arrow) or another tool to stop.

Removing all links from pages

Choose Link ▾ ▸ Remove Links…, type the pages in Remove every link on pages, and click Remove.

The Eraser

The Eraser removes whatever you drag over: letters, parts of pictures, and lines and boxes it touches. What's under them shows through (it doesn't paint white). The page's background stays.

  1. Click Eraser on the Edit PDF bar. The pointer becomes a circle the size of the eraser, a Width box appears beside it on the bar, and anything selected is let go.
  2. Drag over what you want to remove. Each stroke is one undo step.

To change its size, type or pick a width in the Width box (2 to 64 points), click the ▾ beside Eraser and choose Fine (4 pt), Small (8 pt), Medium (12 pt), Large (24 pt), or Huge (48 pt), or press + and - while it's on (2 to 64 points). The Eraser stays on until you choose another tool.

Note: A shape the Eraser touches is removed whole, not cut. To remove sensitive information for good, use redaction instead (see Protecting and Redacting).

Fonts when you save

Some PDFs only name their fonts instead of carrying them, so they look different on computers without those fonts. When you save, PlanetPDF puts any such font that is installed on your computer into the file, cut down to the letters used, so the document looks the same everywhere. The standard fonts every reader has (Helvetica, Times, Courier, Symbol, ZapfDingbats) are left as they are, and so are fonts used by form fields.

Fonts you choose on the text format bar are put into the file the same way. After saving, the status bar tells you about fonts that aren't on this computer and couldn't be added.

A digitally signed document is saved without changing its fonts, so the signatures stay valid.

What Edit PDF can't change

  • Scanned pages are pictures of paper: there is no text to edit, only a picture. Recognizing the text (Recognize Text (OCR)… in the panel) makes it searchable, but that recognized text is invisible and isn't offered for editing. To fix recognition mistakes, use Recognize Text ▾ ▸ Correct Recognized Text… on the Scan & OCR bar (see Scans, Text Recognition, and File Size).
  • Text drawn as outlines (some design programs turn letters into shapes) can't be edited as text; its letters appear as shapes you can move or delete. Recognize Text makes it searchable and copyable, but not editable.
  • Comments, notes, and form fields aren't page text: change them with the Comment and Prepare Form tools.
  • Each line is edited on its own: paragraphs don't rewrap.
