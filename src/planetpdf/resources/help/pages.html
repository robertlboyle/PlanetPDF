Organizing Pages

This chapter covers how to arrange a document's pages: putting them in a new order, adding and deleting pages, turning, cropping, splitting and combining files, numbering pages, adding headers, footers, watermarks, and backgrounds, and making bookmarks. Every change here can be undone with Edit ▸ Undo (Ctrl+Z) until you close the document.

Typing page numbers

Many commands in this chapter have a Pages box. It starts with the pages you have selected (or all). You can type:

You typeIt means
allEvery page.
3Page 3 only.
1,5,8-12Pages 1, 5, and 8 through 12.
5-Page 5 to the end.
-3Pages 1 through 3.
12-8Pages 12 down to 8, in that order.

Page numbers are counted from 1, the way they appear under the thumbnails. When the order matters (for example, when you extract pages), PlanetPDF keeps the order you typed. A page typed twice is used once.

Selecting pages

Commands that work on "the selected pages" use the pages selected in the Pages panel or in the Organize Pages grid. If no page is selected, they work on the page you are looking at.

  • Click a page to select it.
  • Ctrl+click adds a page to the selection or takes it out.
  • Shift+click selects every page from the last one you clicked to this one.

To show the Pages panel, choose View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Page Thumbnails, click the Pages button at the edge of the window, or press F4 (which shows or hides the navigation panes). Right-click a thumbnail for Rotate Clockwise 90°, Rotate Counterclockwise 90°, Rotate Pages…, Insert Blank Page, Insert Pages from File…, Extract Pages…, and Delete Selected Pages.

The Organize Pages tool

Organize Pages shows every page of the document as a large tile in place of the page view, so you can see the whole document at once and rearrange it.

  1. Choose Tools ▸ Organize Pages, or click Organize Pages on the tool strip.
  2. The pages appear as a grid. The Organize Pages bar appears under the main toolbar.
  3. When you are done, click Close at the right end of the bar. Double-clicking a tile also closes the grid and opens that page.

Buttons on a tile

When you point at a tile, three small buttons appear on it: rotate counterclockwise, rotate clockwise, and delete page. They act on that one page.

Putting pages in a different order

You can drag pages in the Organize Pages grid or in the Pages panel.

  1. Select the pages to move (one page, or several with Ctrl or Shift).
  2. Press on one of them and drag. A line shows where the pages will go.
  3. Let go of the mouse button. The pages move there, in their original order.

Press Esc while dragging to cancel.

Copying pages by dragging

Hold Shift when you let go of the mouse button: copies of the pages go there, and the originals stay where they were. The copies keep their comments.

Dragging pages into another document

When two documents are open in separate windows (see Window ▸ Move to New Window), you can drag pages from one document's Pages panel or Organize Pages grid into the other window:

  • Drop them on the other window's Pages panel or Organize Pages grid: a line shows where they will go.
  • Drop them on the other window's page view: they go before or after the page under the mouse.

This always copies the pages; the first document doesn't change. In the receiving document the copy is one undo step, named "Copy N Pages from" the other file.

Note: If the document is digitally signed, PlanetPDF warns you once that adding, removing, or moving pages invalidates its signatures, and asks whether to continue.

Inserting pages

New pages go after the last selected page (or after the page you are looking at). You can insert pages from several places:

Where fromHow
Another PDFFile ▸ Insert Pages ▸ From File… (Shift+Ctrl+I). Every page of the chosen PDF is inserted. If the file has a password, PlanetPDF asks for it.
The clipboardFile ▸ Insert Pages ▸ From Clipboard…. A picture on the clipboard becomes a page; copied text becomes pages of text.
A scannerFile ▸ Insert Pages ▸ From Scanner…. See Scans, Text Recognition, and File Size.
A web pageFile ▸ Insert Pages ▸ From Web Page…. Needs Microsoft Edge or Google Chrome. See Creating, Converting, and Comparing.
A blank pageFile ▸ Insert Pages ▸ A Blank Page…. The new page is the same size as the page next to it.

The same commands are on the Organize Pages bar under Insert ▾, and the Scan & OCR bar has Insert ▾ with From File… and From Scanner.

Inserting by dropping files

Drag PDF files from File Explorer (Finder on a Mac) onto the Pages panel or the Organize Pages grid. Their pages are inserted where you drop them, between the two pages on either side of the drop line. Dropping a file anywhere else in the window opens it as a new document instead.

Deleting pages

  1. Select the pages in the Pages panel or the Organize Pages grid.
  2. Choose Edit ▸ Delete Pages (Shift+Ctrl+D), press Delete while the Pages panel or grid has the focus, or click the trash can on the Organize Pages or Scan & OCR bar.

A PDF must keep at least one page, so you can't delete every page. Use Edit ▸ Undo to bring deleted pages back.

Rotating pages

Rotating a page changes the document: the page stays turned when you save, print, or open it in another program. Nothing on the page is redrawn or loses quality.

  • Edit ▸ Rotate Clockwise and Edit ▸ Rotate Counterclockwise turn the selected pages (or the current page) 90°. The same buttons are on the Organize Pages and Scan & OCR bars.
  • Rotate Pages… (Shift+Ctrl+R, also under More ▾ on the Organize Pages bar) opens a dialog: choose Selected pages, All pages, or These pages, then the Direction (Clockwise or Counterclockwise) and the Angle (90°, 180°, or 270°), and click OK.

Rotate View is different

View ▸ Rotate View ▸ Clockwise (Shift+Ctrl+Plus) and Counterclockwise (Shift+Ctrl+Minus) only turn how the pages are shown on your screen, for reading. The file isn't changed, and nothing needs saving. Use Rotate Pages when the page itself is the wrong way up.

Extracting pages

Extracting saves some pages as a new PDF. The open document isn't changed, and unsaved changes are included in the new file.

  1. Select the pages, then click Extract on the Organize Pages bar (or right-click a thumbnail and choose Extract Pages…).
  2. Check the Pages box. The pages go into the new file in the order you type them.
  3. Click OK, choose a name and folder (PlanetPDF suggests the document's name with "_pages"), and save.

If the document is password protected, PlanetPDF asks whether to protect the new file too.

Splitting a document

Splitting writes the document out as several smaller PDFs. The open document isn't changed.

  1. Click Split on the Organize Pages bar.
  2. Choose how to split:
    • Every N page(s) per file: for example 1 makes one file per page.
    • These groups: type the groups separated by semicolons, for example 1-3; 4-10; 11-.
    • At each top-level bookmark: each file runs from one top-level bookmark to the next (only when the document has bookmarks). Pages before the first bookmark go into a file of their own.
  3. Click Choose Folder… and pick the folder for the new files.

The files are named after the document with a number (and the bookmark's title when you split at bookmarks), for example Report_01.pdf, Report_02 - Introduction.pdf. The window shows "Writing file 2 of 5" as it works; you can cancel it. If the document is protected, PlanetPDF asks whether to protect the new files too.

Replacing pages

Replace Pages swaps some pages of this document for pages of another PDF, for example a corrected page.

  1. Click Replace on the Organize Pages bar.
  2. In Replace pages, type the pages to take out.
  3. Click Choose File… and pick the other PDF.
  4. In Its pages, type which of its pages to use (all, or for example 1-3).
  5. Click Replace.

The new pages go where the first replaced page was. The numbers don't have to match: you can replace two pages with five. It is one undo step.

Cropping pages

Cropping hides the edges of a page. Nothing is deleted: the hidden part is still in the file, and the crop can be undone.

With the Crop Pages dialog

  1. Choose Crop Pages… (Shift+Ctrl+T): it's under More ▾ on the Organize Pages bar and is the Crop page button on the Edit PDF bar.
  2. Enter how much to trim from each side in inches: Trim left, Trim top, Trim right, Trim bottom.
  3. Check the Pages box and click Crop.

To draw the area instead, click Draw Crop Box on the Page Instead…, then drag a box around the part of the page to keep. Esc cancels.

By drawing a box (Scan & OCR)

  1. Open Tools ▸ Scan & OCR and click the Crop Pages button.
  2. Drag a box around the part of the page to keep. That page is cropped at once.

The tool turns off after one crop. To crop many pages the same way, use the Crop Pages dialog.

Page labels

Page labels change how pages are numbered in the Pages panel and in other PDF readers' page boxes, for example i–iv for the front matter and then 1, 2, 3. The pages themselves don't change, and nothing is printed on them (for printed numbers, see Page numbers).

  1. Choose Page Labels… under More ▾ on the Organize Pages bar.
  2. Each row is a range that runs until the next row's first page. Set:
    • From page: the range's first page.
    • Style: 1, 2, 3 · i, ii, iii · I, II, III · a, b, c · A, B, C · or None (prefix only).
    • Prefix: text in front of the number, for example A-.
    • Start at: the number of the range's first page.
  3. Use Add Range and Remove Range as needed, or Remove All Labels to number the pages 1, 2, 3 again. Click OK.

Combining files

Combine Files makes one new PDF from several PDFs and pictures.

  1. Choose File ▸ Create ▸ Combine Files into a Single PDF… (the Create PDF tool's Multiple Files choice does the same).
  2. Click Add Files… and choose PDFs and pictures. Add more as needed.
  3. Put them in order: drag them in the list, or use Move Up and Move Down. Remove takes a file out of the list.
  4. For pictures, choose Image pages: Same size as the image, Letter (fit the image), or A4 (fit the image).
  5. Tick Recognize text (OCR) so the result is searchable if the pictures are scans and you want to search them (only when text recognition is installed).
  6. Click Create PDF…, choose a name and folder, and save.

The window shows "Adding file 2 of 6" as it works. If a PDF has a password, PlanetPDF asks for it. The new PDF opens when it's done.

Tip: To add pages from another file to the document already open, use Inserting pages instead.

Headers, footers, page numbers, Bates numbers, watermarks, and backgrounds

These add text or a picture to many pages at once. They become part of each page, so every viewer and printer shows them. PlanetPDF remembers the ones it added, so you can change them later with Update… or take them off with Remove….

You find them in several places:

  • Edit PDF bar: Header & Footer ▾ (Add…, Update…, Remove…), Watermark ▾ (Add…, Update…, Remove…), and More ▾ with Add Page Numbers…, Bates Numbering…, and Background ▸ (Add…, Update…, Remove…).
  • Organize Pages bar, More ▾: Add Page Numbers… and Header & Footer….
  • Stamp and Scan & OCR bars: Bates Numbering ▾ with Add… and Remove….

Note: Update and Remove only find headers, watermarks, and so on that this version of PlanetPDF added. Ones added by other programs (or by an earlier PlanetPDF) are part of the page content and can't be told apart from it; PlanetPDF says so when it finds none.

Header & footer

  1. Choose Header & Footer ▾ ▸ Add….
  2. Type text in any of the six boxes: Top left, Top center, Top right, Bottom left, Bottom center, Bottom right.
  3. Use these fields in the text; they are filled in on each page:
    FieldBecomes
    {page}The page number.
    {pages}The total number of pages.
    {bates}The Bates number.
    {date}Today's date.
    {file}The file name.
  4. Set the look: Font (Helvetica or Times) and Bold, Size, Color, Distance from edge (inches), and First page number (the number {page} starts at).
  5. Check the Pages box and click Add.

To change it later, choose Header & Footer ▾ ▸ Update…: the dialog opens with the settings you used; change them and click Update. Header & Footer ▾ ▸ Remove… takes it off every page.

Page numbers

Add Page Numbers… opens the same dialog with Page {page} of {pages} already in Bottom center. Change the text or position if you like, and click Add. Page numbers count as a header and footer: change or remove them with Header & Footer ▾ ▸ Update… or Remove….

Bates numbering

Bates numbers give every page a unique number, as used for legal documents.

  1. Choose Bates Numbering… (or Bates Numbering ▾ ▸ Add…). The dialog puts {bates} in Bottom right.
  2. Under Bates numbering ({bates}), set the Prefix (for example PN-), Suffix, Start at, and Digits (6 digits makes PN-000001).
  3. Check the Pages box and click Add.

Bates Numbering ▾ ▸ Remove… takes PlanetPDF's Bates numbers off every page.

Watermark

A watermark is large, faint text or a picture across the page, usually behind the content. (A stamp, by contrast, sits on top: see Comments, Notes, and Stamps.)

  1. Choose Watermark ▾ ▸ Add….
  2. Choose Text: and type it (it starts as CONFIDENTIAL), or choose Image: and click Choose… for a PNG or JPEG picture.
  3. Set the Color (for text), Opacity, Rotation (−90° to 90°), and Size (a percentage of the page width).
  4. Behind the page content is ticked: untick it to put the watermark on top.
  5. Check the Pages box and click Add.

Watermark ▾ ▸ Update… changes it; Watermark ▾ ▸ Remove… takes it off.

Background

A background is a color or a picture under everything on the page.

  1. On the Edit PDF bar, choose More ▾ ▸ Background ▸ Add….
  2. Choose Color: and pick one, or Picture: and click Choose…. For a picture, set the Picture size (a percentage of the page).
  3. Set the Opacity, check the Pages box, and click Add.

Background ▸ Update… changes it; Background ▸ Remove… takes it off.

Bookmarks

Bookmarks are a clickable list of a document's sections. They show in the Bookmarks panel here and in every other PDF reader. To show the panel, click the Bookmarks button at the edge of the window or choose View ▸ Show/Hide ▸ Navigation Panes ▸ Bookmarks. A file that has bookmarks opens with the panel showing.

Click a bookmark to go to its place. Bookmarks with others under them can be opened and closed with the small arrow beside them.

The Bookmarks tool

The quickest way to make bookmarks is to select a heading and press Enter.

  1. Choose Tools ▸ Bookmarks, or click Bookmarks on the tool strip. The pointer becomes the I-beam for selecting text, and the Bookmarks panel opens.
  2. Drag over a heading on the page to select it.
  3. Press Enter or +, click Add Bookmark on the bar, or right-click and choose Add Bookmark.

The bookmark takes the selected text as its title (up to 120 characters) and goes to that spot on the page. It is placed in reading order among the other bookmarks, at the same level as the bookmark before it. With no text selected, Add Bookmark bookmarks the current page.

Tip: Right-click ▸ Add Bookmark on selected text works with any tool, not only the Bookmarks tool.

Adding bookmarks automatically

Add Bookmarks Automatically… on the Bookmarks bar looks through the document for its sections.

  1. Click Add Bookmarks Automatically…. PlanetPDF first looks for a table of contents in the first pages (lines ending in page numbers, often with dot leaders). Without one, it looks for headings: lines in larger or bold type such as "Chapter 3" or "2.1 Scope", skipping text repeated at the top of every page.
  2. A list of what it found opens, with levels nested under each other. Untick any you don't want.
  3. If the document already has bookmarks, choose Replace the N bookmarks already here or Add them to the bookmarks already here.
  4. Click Add Bookmarks. All of them are one undo step.

If nothing is found, PlanetPDF says so; select the headings and press Enter to bookmark them yourself.

Adding, renaming, and deleting in the panel

  • Add (at the bottom of the panel) bookmarks the current page and lets you type its title in the list.
  • Rename: select a bookmark and press F2, or right-click it and choose Rename. Type the new title and press Enter. A plain click never renames; it only jumps.
  • Set Destination: to point a bookmark somewhere else, select text on the page (or go to the page), then right-click the bookmark and choose Set Destination. It now goes to the selected text, or to the top of the page shown. Its title, color, and style stay.
  • Delete: select it and click Delete in the panel or Delete Bookmark on the Bookmarks bar, or right-click ▸ Delete. Bookmarks under it are deleted too.
  • Right-click also offers Go to Bookmark and Add Bookmark.

Renaming and adding never move the other bookmarks: each keeps exactly the place it had.
